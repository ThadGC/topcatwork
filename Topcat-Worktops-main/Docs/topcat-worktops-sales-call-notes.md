# Topcat Worktops — Sales Call Notes (Summary)

**Source:** Luke's notes from the recorded Topcat Worktops sales call (run by Thad).
**Purpose:** A clean, structured record of what we learned about the business and the people running it, to inform the website and marketing build.
**Companion to:** the Industry briefing and the Customer Psychology briefing.

---

## 1. What Topcat is

- **Service offered:** fabrication + installation of stone worktops.
- **Operational reality:** they **outsource the fabrication** and focus on sales, installation, and project management. The cutting/fabrication is done through Zaman's industry connections, not in-house. This is the core of how their economics work (see Section 3).

## 2. The people behind it

- The owners **currently have day jobs** and want to **go all in** on this business. Growing it enough to do that full-time is a key motivation.
- **Zaman:** a distributor in the industry. His connections are what make the outsourced-fabrication model possible.
- They started the business after seeing that **competitors overcharge**, and built a model around undercutting them using Zaman's connections.

## 3. Business model & economics (the key numbers)

- **Average job = a 3-slab job.**
- **Competitor price** for that job: roughly **£3.8k to £4k**.
- **Topcat price:** roughly **£3k to £3.2k**.
- **They undercut competitors by ~£500** (roughly £600 to £700).
- They **think in absolute profit per job, not percentage margin.** Target profit is roughly **£750 to £1,000 per job**.
- **Lower overhead than competitors** because fabrication is outsourced.

**Volume / momentum:**
- Average **2 to 3 jobs per month**.
- Just had their **busiest month**, which is what prompted them to invest in marketing.
- The **previous ~4 months were almost nothing.**

## 4. Positioning

- **Outside impression they want to project:** experienced, with a good portfolio. They **sometimes come in higher** than the cheapest, so they are not positioning purely as the budget option.
- Brand/site standard: **everything must look pristine, clean, and professional.**

## 5. Goals for the engagement

- **Expand reach across the UK.**
- **Attract more B2B clients** (they bring the big jobs) and have the site **cater to them**, while still keeping their **normal homeowner clients**.
- Build something that helps the business grow to the point the owners can go full-time.

## 6. Competitors

- Named competitors: **Emi Stone, Roma Marble, Stonetech (cheap)**. (Spellings taken from handwriting; worth verifying.)
- Luke's view: **competition is poor, all the same, nothing special.** They see room to stand out.

## 7. Marketing history & what they need from us

- They have **worked with bad marketing agencies before** and were burned.
- They need confidence they will **get out what they put in** (clear ROI).
- Their **current site has brought in only 1 client in 9 months**, so it is effectively not working.


## 8. Website requirements & preferences

- Likes **trust badges** on the site.
- Likes the idea of a **"wheel"** for browsing the different stones (a stone selector).
- **Everything must look pristine, clean, and beautiful** (premium feel).


---

## 9. Takeaways & flags worth raising

These connect the call notes to the earlier industry and customer research. A few of them protect us from easy mistakes.

1. **Do not claim "in-house fabrication" anywhere on the site.** This is the important one. Many worktop sites use "in-house fabrication" and "single point of accountability" as trust signals, and buyers and trade genuinely value it. **Topcat outsources fabrication.** Claiming otherwise would be false and risky. Frame their model around what is true and still strong: trusted, curated fabrication partners, plus their own installation, oversight, and home service.

2. **Resolve the price-versus-premium tension before writing copy.** Their economics run on undercutting and thin per-job profit (£750 to £1,000), but the impression they want is experienced and premium, they sometimes price higher, and they avoid advertising on price. A site that screams "cheapest" would undercut both the premium impression and the B2B trust play. Recommendation: lead on value, craft, convenience (home service), and reliability, and let the price advantage be a pleasant discovery at quote stage, not the headline.

3. **Build the quote as a lead-capture, not a price calculator.** This matches their explicit request (upload dimensions, routed to them, they quote) and fits their per-job pricing flexibility. It also means "Get a Quote" is really "Start an enquiry," which is fine, but the copy should set that expectation so users are not waiting for an instant number.

4. **They will judge us on measurable leads, not traffic.** Given the bad-agency history and the current site converting ~1 client in 9 months, set up conversion tracking from day one (quote submissions and calls) and make homeowner and B2B lead generation the headline metric, not vanity traffic.

5. **The B2B push is the client's stated priority, but keep the margin caution in view.** Per-job profit is already thin and undercutting is the play, so chasing big B2B jobs at thin margins can become a volume treadmill. Worth confirming with them whether the real goal is margin or volume. Either way, the Trade audience should still get its own clearly signposted path on the site (per the customer-psychology brief), not a blended message.

6. **Safety/silica sits with their fabrication partners, and that is usable.** Because fabrication is outsourced, the occupational silica risk and its reputational fallout attach to whoever cuts the stone. Using WFF-accredited or demonstrably compliant fabrication partners is both genuinely important and a credible trust signal we can use, without ever implying Topcat cuts stone themselves.
