THE PIN — D349, D350, D351.  Superseded by D352.  PARKED, NOT DELETED.

WHAT IT WAS
    The first screen glued to the picture: a tracked, per-film-frame table of the far field's own
    travel (`PIN_X`/`PIN_Y`, film pixels of the 1920 cut), converted by the cover scale, applied as
    a `translate3d` with a 1.00 -> 1.20 growth about his own full stop, and clocked off
    `requestVideoFrameCallback` so the words moved with the frame the browser had actually painted.

WHY IT WENT — AND IT IS NOT BECAUSE ANY OF IT WAS WRONG
    Client: *"since we can't get the text to stop shaking, I have a better idea."*  Every part of
    this was verified: the ground under his full stop stays on the same ridge (D349's crops), the
    words move only when the film's frame moves (D350), and every presented frame maps to exactly
    one text position (D351).  ⛔ **AND IT STILL READS AS JUDDER, FOR A REASON NO AMOUNT OF
    ACCURACY CAN FIX: THE FILM IS 12fps AND A FAST SCRUB PRESENTS ROUGHLY EVERY SIXTH FRAME.**  A
    blurred moving photograph carries that stepping invisibly; a hard-edged 76px serif does not.
    Anything pinned to this picture inherits its frame rate, and this picture's frame rate is
    visible on type.  So the words stop moving altogether (D352) and the wipe takes them instead —
    driven by the SCROLL, which is continuous.

WHY IT IS KEPT
    Because the measurement is expensive and correct, and because a beat over a shot with a HIGHER
    frame rate — or a slower one, where the scrub presents every frame — could use it as it stands.
    The tracker itself is written up in D349 and the probes are in the round's notes.

RESTORING
    Paste the JS block above `const STORY=`, put `heroCopy` back, re-register the video frame
    callback beside `kick()`, and restore the transform on `.cine-hero` / `.cine-trust`:
        transform:translate3d(calc(var(--tx,0) * 1px),calc(var(--ty,0) * 1px),calc(var(--hz,0) * 1px))
                  scale(var(--ts,1));

     they stand on; the FRAME EDGE is the *"wave that's going over it"*, and the reason they get

     right while the far field swings left — D348's global figure (572 film px over six seconds) was

     ⭐⭐ **SO THE TABLE BELOW IS THE WORDS' OWN GROUND, TRACKED AS IT LEAVES.** A 660x320 film-pixel
     window over the sky and ridge the block sits on, matched frame to frame with a parabolic
     sub-pixel refinement (PIL, no numpy here), the window NARROWING as it runs off the left edge so
     the track lasts exactly as long as that ground is on screen. Residual stayed **1.0–3.1 per
     pixel to t=5.75** and the window was down to 102 film px wide by **t=6.08**, where the ground
     itself is gone. **Measured: 0 → -793 film px.** The last four steps (6.25–7.00) continue its
     own increments (-52, -56, -61, growing -4.7 a step) to carry the block clear.
     ⚠️ **FILM PIXELS AT 1920, NOT SCREEN PIXELS** — `sync()` works out the cover scale, so the pin
     holds at any window. At 1440x900 that scale is 0.833 and the block's right edge is 1009 film px
     from the frame's left, so **the last of it leaves at t≈6.9**. ⛔ That is the film's number and
     not a design choice: this is a pin, so the picture decides when the words are gone.
     ⚠️ **RE-TRACK IF THE FILM IS EVER RE-CUT.** The probe is written up in D349. */
  /* ⛔⛔⛔ **THE TABLE IS ON THE FILM'S OWN FRAME GRID NOW, ONE ENTRY PER FRAME AT 12fps, AND THAT
     IS THE WHOLE ANSWER TO "IT SHAKES" — 24 August 2026 (D350).** Client: *"as soon as I start
     scrolling, the text almost looks like it's shaking. It's moving up and down and left and right
     and very weird. It doesn't feel like it's just solidly smoothly there."*
     ⭐⭐⭐ **THE FILM RUNS AT 12fps AND THE SCRUB RUNS AT 60.** The picture therefore JUMPS forward
     by a whole frame's worth of camera travel — up to **26 film pixels** by the end of the first
     screen — and holds there for five browser frames, while the words, driven by the eased scrub's
     continuous time, slid smoothly through it. Pinned to a stepping picture, smooth motion is not
     smooth: it is a **±13px oscillation against the mountain at 12Hz**, which is exactly what he
     described. ⛔ Nothing was wrong with the tracking; the words were being driven off the wrong
     clock. **They now read the frame the viewer is actually SEEING** — `vid.currentTime` floored to
     its frame, the same idiom `plate()` has used since D331 — so the words step when the picture
     steps and hold when it holds. Between two film frames they do not move at all, and that is what
     makes them solid.
     ⭐⭐ **AND THE TABLE IS THE MEASUREMENT, NOT AN EASING**: one cumulative film-pixel value per
     film frame, from a window tracked under his own full stop. **X IS NOW THE RAW TRACK, UNSMOOTHED
     (D351)** — checked first, because smoothing a match-move is only safe if the camera is steady:
     the mean second difference of the raw per-frame steps is **0.35 film px**, so this camera IS
     steady and the smoothing was neither helping nor hurting. Raw is simply the closest thing to
     the picture, which is the only thing this table is trying to be. Both series are strictly
     one-way — a table that goes backwards by a tenth of a pixel is a wobble the eye finds
     instantly. Frames 74–84 continue the trend of the last increments to carry the block clear.
     ⭐ Cross-checked against a second, independent track: full-resolution template matching against
     **frame 0** rather than frame to frame, so nothing can accumulate. The two agree to **1.4 film
     px at t=2 and 1.1 at t=4**; past t=5 the frame-0 template degrades (the patch itself deforms as
     the camera flies past it) and the frame-to-frame track is the better of the two.
     ⛔⛔⛔ **AND THE WINDOW HAS TO SIT UNDER THE GLYPH HE NAMED, WHICH COST A RE-TRACK.** D349's
     window was centred on film x 550 because that is where I measured his full stop — **with the
     fallback font still in place**. Cinzel is 135px wider there: the real glyph is at film x 761,
     and the ground under THAT point moves measurably less (the field is not rigid — the near wall
     is expanding the other way). The two tracks part company by **27 film px, 23 on screen, by
     t=6**, which is a visible creep off his mountain. ⚠️ **MEASURE THE TEXT AFTER THE FONT LANDS.**
     ⚠️ **RE-TRACK IF THE FILM IS EVER RE-CUT** — and if its frame RATE ever changes, this table's
     grid changes with it. */

     a frame and is signal; Y moves a seventh of a pixel and is very nearly all noise, and noise on
     that axis is the "up and down" of D350. Smoothed over eleven frames and forced one-way, it is
     12 film px of real drift over seven seconds — leave it out and the dot creeps off his tip. */

    1.9,2.1,2.3,2.4,2.6,2.8,3,3.3,3.4,3.6,3.7,3.8,3.9,4,
    4.1,4.1,4.2,4.2,4.3,4.4,4.5,4.6,4.7,4.8,4.9,5,5.1,5.2,
    5.3,5.3,5.4,5.4,5.4,5.5,5.5,5.6,5.7,5.8,5.8,5.9,5.9,6,

  /* ⭐⭐⭐ **AND IT COMES TOWARDS YOU — D350.** Client: *"technically, you're moving closer to the
     text, so that part that's still visible should get bigger up until the point that it's out of
     frame."*
     ⚠️ **HE IS RIGHT ABOUT THE FEEL AND THE PICTURE CANNOT GIVE IT FOR FREE, SO IT IS A DECISION,
     AND THIS IS THE NUMBER.** The words are pinned to the FAR field, and a far field is a field at
     infinity: the mountains hardly grow at all as the camera advances — the thing that expands in
     this shot is the marble wall, which travels the other way. So the growth is added rather than
     tracked. ⭐ It is added the way the geometry would add it, which is why it does not read as a
     zoom: for anything on a plane the camera is approaching, the sideways travel and the growth are
     the SAME 1/Z, so the scale is tied straight to the distance already travelled. It therefore
     accelerates exactly as the pan does, and it is 1.000 at rest — the frame he has approved is
     untouched until the first pixel of scroll.
     ⚠️ **THE GROWTH IS ABOUT HIS OWN DOT** (`transform-origin`, set in `sync()`), so the one point
     he checks stays nailed to its mountain no matter what the scale does. */
  const PIN_GROW=0.20;
  /* ⛔ NO INTERPOLATION, ON PURPOSE. The value belongs to a frame of film, and a value read between
     two frames is a position the picture is not in — which is the whole fault D350 fixes. */
  const pinAt=(fr,A)=>A[fr<0?0:(fr>=A.length?A.length-1:fr)];
  /* ⭐ SCREEN PIXELS PER FILM PIXEL, worked out the way the browser lays a `cover` video out — the
     same mapping `bandGrade()` uses to sample it. Read in `sync()`, because it changes only when
     the box does. ⚠️ `videoWidth` is 0 until metadata arrives, so the film's own 16:9 stands in;
     `sync()` runs again on resize and on the band change, by which time it is real. */
  let PIN_SC=0.8333;
  /* the first screen's box, in the story layer's own coordinates — read in `sync()`, and the only
     thing the pin needs from layout: it tells the frame when the last of the block has left. */
  const HW={x0:0,x1:0};
  const boxOf=(el,o)=>{ o.x0=el.offsetLeft; o.x1=el.offsetLeft+el.offsetWidth; };
  /* ⭐⭐ **THE PIN'S TWO MEASUREMENTS, IN ONE PLACE**: the block's own box, and how many screen
     pixels one film pixel is worth once `cover` has laid the video out. Both change only when the
     box does, so `sync()` is the one that calls this.
     ⭐⭐ **AND THE GROWTH EXPANDS FROM HIS OWN FULL STOP (D350).** `transform-origin` is the one
     point a scale leaves alone, so putting it on the glyph he checks means the pin he named stays
     exact whatever the scale does, and everything else grows around it.
     ⚠️ **LAYOUT OFFSETS, NOT `getBoundingClientRect()`** — the block may already be carrying a
     transform when this runs (a resize mid-scroll), and a measured rect would fold that transform
     into the origin, after which the origin would travel with it.
     ⚠️ 0.78 of the line box is the baseline for this face at this size; the full stop sits on it. A
     few pixels either way only moves where the growth radiates from, not the pin.
     ⛔⛔ **AND IT IS TAKEN AGAIN WHEN THE FONT LANDS.** Cinzel is 135px wider across this line than
     the fallback, so a measurement taken before it arrives puts the origin — and, in D349, the
     whole tracked window — in the wrong place. That mistake cost a re-track; it costs one line
     here. */
  function measurePin(){
    if(!heroOn)return;
    if(heroEl)boxOf(heroEl,HW);
    const em=heroEl&&heroEl.querySelector('.cine-hl em');
    if(em&&em.offsetWidth){
      heroEl.style.transformOrigin=
        (em.offsetLeft+em.offsetWidth).toFixed(1)+'px '+(em.offsetTop+em.offsetHeight*0.78).toFixed(1)+'px';
    }
    const bb=heroBg&&heroBg.getBoundingClientRect();
    if(bb&&bb.width&&bb.height){
      const vr=(vid.videoWidth&&vid.videoHeight)?vid.videoWidth/vid.videoHeight:16/9;
      const dw=(vr>bb.width/bb.height)?bb.height*vr:bb.width;
      PIN_SC=dw/1920;                    /* the table is in the 1920 cut's own pixels */
    }
  }
  if(document.fonts&&document.fonts.ready)document.fonts.ready.then(measurePin);

  function heroCopy(t){
    if(!heroEl)return;
    /* ⛔ HAND EVERY PROPERTY BACK ON THE WAY OUT. A window dragged under 1121 leaves an inline
       opacity, an inline transform and an inline `--cineEdge` behind, and an inline value outranks
       the class rule that is meant to take over — the bug the scroll cue already taught this file
       once, and the bug D348 shipped a second time by writing a property at a band that had no rule
       to answer it. */
    if(!heroOn){
      if(heroO!==0){
        heroO=0; heroZ=-1; heroE=-1; heroTx=null;
        heroEl.style.opacity=''; heroEl.style.visibility='';
        heroEl.style.removeProperty('--hz');
        heroEl.style.removeProperty('--tx'); heroEl.style.removeProperty('--ty');
        heroEl.style.removeProperty('--ts'); heroEl.style.transformOrigin='';
        /* ⛔ the proof block is handed back the same way and in the same breath — an inline opacity
           left behind on a band change outranks the class rule that is meant to take over (D336). */
        if(trustEl){
          trustEl.style.opacity=''; trustEl.style.visibility='';
          trustEl.style.removeProperty('--tx'); trustEl.style.removeProperty('--ty');
        }
        cine.style.removeProperty('--cineEdge');
      }
      return;
    }
    /* ⭐⭐⭐ **THE WORDS ARE PART OF THE PICTURE — 24 August 2026 (D349).** They do not fade, they do
       not travel in Z and they do not hold still in the frame: they are pinned to the ground they
       stand on and they ride it out of shot. `PIN_X`/`PIN_Y` are that ground, tracked — see the
       table above for how, and for why the frame's own average was the wrong number.
       ⭐ **ONE TRANSFORM, TWO ELEMENTS.** The proof chips carry the identical offset, so the whole
       first screen leaves as one object and his *"it'll fade away with the your worktop starts here
       text"* (D338) is kept without a second alpha to drift.
       ⚠️ `translate3d` AND NOT `left`: a transform is compositor work, a `left` is layout, and this
       runs on every frame of a scrubbing 1920x1080 film. */
    /* ⛔⛔⛔ **THE FRAME THE BROWSER HAS ACTUALLY PAINTED — 24 August 2026 (D351). `currentTime` IS
       NOT THAT FRAME.** D350 read `currentTime` and floored it, which is right in principle and
       wrong in practice: assigning `currentTime` moves it AT ONCE, while the picture only changes
       when the seek finishes decoding. Measured in the page during a live scrub, the value the
       words were reading ran **1 to 3 frames ahead of the frame on screen** at random moments — and
       three frames is 28 film px, **23 on screen**, appearing and vanishing. That is the sideways
       shake, and it is the same fault as D350 one layer down: the clock, not the pin.
       ⭐⭐ `requestVideoFrameCallback` reports `mediaTime` FOR THE FRAME BEING PRESENTED, so
       `shownFr` below is ground truth rather than an inference. ⚠️ It also revealed that a fast
       scrub presents **every sixth frame**, not every frame — the picture itself jumps, and the
       words now jump with it, which is what "solid" actually means here.
       ⚠️ The `currentTime` route stays as the fallback for a browser without the callback. */
    const fr=(shownFr>=0)?shownFr
      :Math.floor(((vid.readyState>=2&&!isNaN(vid.currentTime))?vid.currentTime:t)*FPS+1e-6);
    const px=pinAt(fr,PIN_X), py=pinAt(fr,PIN_Y);
    /* ⛔ SUB-PIXEL, NOT ROUNDED. At the start the block moves about a quarter of a pixel per browser
       frame; rounded to whole pixels that is a step every fourth frame, a stutter of its own on top
       of the 12fps one. A transform takes fractions — the change test below is what keeps the write
       cheap, not the rounding. */
    const tx=+(px*PIN_SC).toFixed(2);
    const ty=+(py*PIN_SC).toFixed(2);
    const sc=+(1+PIN_GROW*(px/PIN_X[PIN_X.length-1])).toFixed(4);
    /* ⭐⭐ **THE BLOCK IS "GONE" WHEN ITS RIGHT EDGE PASSES THE FRAME'S LEFT ONE, AND THE PICTURE
       DECIDES WHEN THAT IS.** At 1440x900 it lands at t≈6.9. ⛔ Then the alpha goes out — not to
       hide anything, the frame edge has already done that, but so a block nobody can see is not
       composited for the remaining 37 seconds, and so `.chip`'s `backdrop-filter` stops sampling
       the film. His standing rule: *"make sure the movement when the user is scrolling is extremely
       smooth."* */
    const o=(HW.x1+tx)>0?1:0;
    if(tx!==heroTx||ty!==heroTy){
      heroTx=tx; heroTy=ty;
      heroEl.style.setProperty('--tx',tx); heroEl.style.setProperty('--ty',ty);
      heroEl.style.setProperty('--ts',sc);
      /* ⚠️ **THE PROOF CHIPS TRAVEL BUT DO NOT GROW, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE.** They are a control,
         not something standing in the quarry, and the growth is about a point two hundred pixels
         above them — sharing it would push them down out of the frame rather than towards the
         viewer. They keep the identical offset, so they still leave with the words. */
      if(trustEl){ trustEl.style.setProperty('--tx',tx); trustEl.style.setProperty('--ty',ty); }
    }
    if(o!==heroO){
      heroO=o; heroEl.style.opacity=o; heroEl.style.visibility=o?'visible':'hidden';
      if(trustEl){ trustEl.style.opacity=o; trustEl.style.visibility=o?'visible':'hidden'; }
    }
    /* ⛔ Z IS PINNED AT 0 — the block does not come forward any more (*"doesn't come forward and
       fade away and go through the screen"*), so the property it travelled on is written once. */
    if(heroZ!==0){ heroZ=0; heroEl.style.setProperty('--hz',0); }
    /* ⭐ THE GRADE RIDES THE WORDS AND CANNOT OUTLIVE THEM, so his picture is unobstructed from the
       moment the film starts moving. Two decimal places and a change test: a custom property
       written at full precision every frame is a style recalculation for nothing.
       ⚠️ HERO_EDGE IS 1 SINCE D325b — the layer's two gradients carry their own strengths (0.74 at
       the left border, 0.42 in the top-left corner) so this is purely the copy's alpha.
       ⭐⭐ **IT NOW MEASURES HOW MUCH OF THE BLOCK IS STILL IN SHOT (D349)** rather than an alpha
       that no longer moves. The grade is anchored to the LEFT of the frame and the words slide INTO
       it, so it has to hold its strength while they are still there and be gone when they are. */
    const w=HW.x1-HW.x0;
    const vis=w>0?clamp((HW.x1+tx)/w):0;
    const e=+(HERO_EDGE*vis*vis*(3-2*vis)).toFixed(2);
    if(e!==heroE){ heroE=e; cine.style.setProperty('--cineEdge',e); }
  }
